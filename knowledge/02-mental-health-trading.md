## 1. Pharmacological Impacts on Decision-Making

### Antipsychotics (Quetiapine, Olanzapine, etc.)

**Documented effects:**
- Significant impact on **memory, executive function, attention, processing speed**
- Modulation of dopamine + serotonin → reduced neuroplasticity, generalized cognitive slowing
- 5-HT2A and D2 receptor antagonism alters how you process reinforcement cues (gains/losses)
- **Atypical antipsychotics can cause shifts toward NEGATIVE expected value choices** in clinical risk-decision tasks (vs placebo)

**Translation for your trading:**
- Your processing speed is slower than baseline
- Your "intuition" about probabilities may be miscalibrated
- You will be more drawn to negative-EV decisions during medication-active states
- **Mechanical checklists must replace intuitive processing** — don't trust gut feel

### SSRIs (Escitalopram, etc.)

**Documented effects:**
- Emotional blunting in 40-60% of users (University of Cambridge research)
- Significantly impairs **reinforcement learning**
- You may not fully process the emotional weight of losses → don't learn from them as well

**Translation for your trading:**
- The "lessons" from a losing trade may not stick at full intensity
- **Manual journaling becomes critical** — write what you should be feeling so the brain catches up
- Don't rely on "I'll remember the pain" — you might not

### Action Items

- [ ] Note medication name + dose in your daily journal entry
- [ ] If meds change → pause live trading for 1 week minimum to recalibrate
- [ ] Discuss trading-relevant cognitive effects with psychiatrist
- [ ] **Never stop or reduce meds without psychiatric supervision** — even if you feel "sharper" off them

## 2. Dopamine Cycles & Compulsive Engagement

### The Risk

Recovery from psychosis involves stabilizing **highly sensitive dopamine pathways**. Day trading activates the brain's reward system **identically to cocaine and gambling addiction**.

For your specific brain:
- Intermittent rewards from markets can trigger sudden craving peaks
- Compulsive trading behavior emerges
- "Persistence will guarantee a win" cognitive distortion (already documented in your patterns)
- Higher relapse risk to compulsive states

### The Protection

**Strict structural barriers (not willpower) are mandatory:**
- [ ] Hard daily lockout after 2 trades (no override possible)
- [ ] No demo trading (variable reward without financial pain = stronger conditioning)
- [ ] Phone has zero trading apps
- [ ] Maximum 4 hours chart time per day, even during research phases
- [ ] Sundays = zero charts (sabbath protocol)

### Watch For

These signal dopamine dysregulation returning:
- Need to "check the chart real quick"
- Difficulty sleeping due to thoughts about setups
- Excitement at the thought of trading vs calm anticipation
- Demo trading "for fun"
- Multiple chart layouts open simultaneously

If 2+ of these → reduce trading frequency, talk to psychiatrist.

## 3. Sleep Deprivation Impact

### The Research

- Sleep deprivation **directly distorts probability perception**
- 24 hours of sleep deprivation modulates decision-making neural systems **before behavioral changes are visible** (you don't know you're impaired)
- Predisposes you to take **substantially greater financial risks**
- For psychosis recovery, sleep architecture is **the foundation** — not a recommendation

### Hard Rules

- [ ] **7+ hours of sleep is non-negotiable** before any trading day
- [ ] If you slept poorly → no live trading, replay backtest only
- [ ] No NY session trading past 11 PM IST (you'd be entering trades at 12:30+ AM, exits later, sleep destroyed)
- [ ] Track sleep in your daily journal (hours + quality 1-10)
- [ ] **Sleep is a risk parameter, not a lifestyle choice**

### Trade-Off Acceptance

You will need to skip late NY session opportunities. Yes, this means missing setups. But:
- A missed setup costs $0
- A trade taken with sleep debt costs much more (impaired probability + dopamine miscalibration)
- The London + early NY window (12:30 PM - 9 PM IST) is sufficient

## 4. Identity Separation & Self-Complexity

### The Theory (Patricia Linville, 1987)

**Self-Complexity Theory:** Having multiple distinct aspects of self **buffers against stress**. When one identity domain takes a hit, others remain intact.

When trading performance is fused with identity:
- A loss = ego threat (not a statistical event)
- Bad day = personal failure
- Good day = personal validation
- Either direction is unhealthy

### Your Specific Risk

You currently have multiple identities:
1. ✅ Freelancer (primary income, real career)
2. ✅ Son (lives with mom, contributes to household)
3. ✅ Person in mental health recovery
4. ✅ Friend / social person (One Piece, parties, gym)
5. 🟡 Trader (currently being defined)

**The danger:** "Full-time trader" identity threatens to consume the others. You said it yourself: *"if I close in loss I'll lose confidence as full-time trader."*

### Action Items

- [ ] **Trading is a SKILL you're developing, not your IDENTITY**
- [ ] Refuse the label "trader" until you have 12+ months of consistent profitability
- [ ] When asked what you do: "I'm a freelancer who's also learning to trade"
- [ ] Spend at least 1 hour/day on a non-trading hobby
- [ ] Maintain at least 3 friendships outside trading communities
- [ ] If you find yourself thinking "I AM a trader, this is who I am" → that's the warning

## 5. When Trading Is Contraindicated

### Stop Trading IMMEDIATELY If:

- ❌ Sleeping < 6 hours per night for 2+ days
- ❌ Skipped medication for any reason
- ❌ Manic state (racing thoughts, grandiosity, "I can do anything")
- ❌ Depressive state (hopelessness, "what's the point")
- ❌ Anxiety attacks happening
- ❌ Psychosis symptoms returning (hallucinations, paranoia)
- ❌ Major life stressor (family conflict, financial shock, breakup)
- ❌ Mom or psychiatrist expresses concern
- ❌ You feel "extra confident" or "extra desperate" — both are red flags

### The Re-Entry Protocol

After any pause:
1. Minimum 7 days no charts (full reset)
2. Psychiatrist check-in
3. 3 days of replay backtesting only
4. 1 trade max for first live week
5. Journal mood + sleep daily
6. Mentor approval before normal trading resumes

## 6. The Mentor's Mental Health Protocol

When you arrive for a session, expect these questions BEFORE any trading talk:
1. How are you feeling today? (genuine, not surface)
2. How did you sleep?
3. Did you take your medication today?
4. How's freelancing this week?
5. Any chart compulsion / can't stop / anxiety since last session?

**If 2+ answers are concerning → mental health conversation FIRST. No technical talk.**

This isn't coddling. It's risk management. Trading on a compromised brain has destroyed more accounts than bad systems ever have.

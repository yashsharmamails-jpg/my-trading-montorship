## PART 1 — PSYCHOLOGY (PATTERNS & INTERVENTIONS)

1. Bias Lock


### Scientific Names:

- Confirmation Bias

- Belief Perseverance

- Commitment Escalation


### Most-Cited Source:

Thinking, Fast and Slow — Daniel Kahneman


### Core Insight:

Humans overweight evidence that confirms an existing thesis and underweight contradictory information. Once emotionally invested in a directional narrative, the brain protects identity consistency rather than adapting to changing information.


### Key Interventions:

- Pre-commitment invalidation rules

- Bayesian probability updates

- Opposite-side analysis

- Three-strike reset rule

2. Loss-Day Aversion


### Scientific Names:

- Loss Aversion

- Outcome Orientation


### Most-Cited Source:

Prospect Theory — Kahneman & Tversky


### Core Insight:

Losses feel psychologically larger than equivalent gains, leading traders to seek emotional recovery instead of rational expectancy.


### Key Interventions:

- Process-based scoring

- Hide floating P&L

- Fixed daily stop rules

- Weekly review framework

3. Revenge Sizing


### Scientific Names:

- Risk-Seeking in Losses

- Gambler’s Fallacy


### Most-Cited Source:

Addiction by Design — Natasha Dow Schüll


### Core Insight:

Variable reward systems distort rational sizing and encourage emotionally motivated risk escalation.


### Key Interventions:

- Hard lot caps

- Broker-side restrictions

- Predefined position sizing

- Accountability screenshots


## PART 2 — MENTAL HEALTH × TRADING


### Key Findings:

- Dopamine dysregulation increases impulsivity

- Sleep deprivation worsens probabilistic thinking

- Antipsychotic medications may reduce impulsivity but slow reaction time

- Trading should never become identity or survival validation


### Important Rules:

- Avoid trading under severe sleep deprivation

- Pause trading during medication instability

- Separate self-worth from equity curve

- Use structured routines and checklists

## PART 8 — EDGE & RISK MANAGEMENT


### Expectancy Formula:

Expectancy = (Win Rate × Avg Win) − (Loss Rate × Avg Loss)
